# Synthetic Chest X-Ray Generation for AI Diagnostics

Master dissertation, *Master in Data Science & AI Strategy*, emlyon business school (2026).
Author: Ana Farré Matemalas. Supervisor: Saeed Varasteh Yazdi.

## Research question

To what extent can synthetic chest X-ray images generated by diffusion models and by GANs
improve the performance of AI diagnostic classifiers, particularly for underrepresented
disease classes and in small-data settings, and what are the implications of using them
under the EU regulatory framework (GDPR and the EU AI Act)?

## Overview

Two generators are compared as two practical routes to synthetic data:

| Generator | How it is obtained | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **DC-GAN** (Radford et al., 2016) | Trained from scratch on the real training set, one model per class, 128 px, DiffAugment + EMA | Pneumonia and No Finding images (TB: 1,000 per class) |
| **RoentGen-v2** (Moroianu et al., 2025) | Pretrained Stable Diffusion model on MIMIC-CXR, used zero-shot with text prompts, 512 px | Pneumonia and No Finding images (TB: prompts for tuberculosis and normal) |

The comparison is between two practical routes, not between two architectures trained on
identical data (RoentGen-v2 carries MIMIC-CXR knowledge and was trained on PA views only).

There are two case studies:

1. **Main study: pneumonia** in NIH ChestX-ray14, with a 5,000-image real training set
   (683 positives).
2. **Second case study: tuberculosis** in small datasets: Shenzhen (396 training images after
   the split) for training and internal test, and Montgomery as an external test set.

### Scenarios

| Pneumonia | Tuberculosis | Training data |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | T1 | Real only (baseline) |
| S2 | T2 | Synthetic only, DC-GAN |
| S3 | T3 | Real + DC-GAN positives |
| S4 | T4 | Synthetic only, RoentGen-v2 |
| S5 | T5 | Real + RoentGen-v2 positives |
| S6 | T6 | S6: real 10K (size control). T6: DC-GAN pre-training, then real fine-tuning |
| S3-bal, S5-bal | T3-bal, T5-bal | Also add synthetic negatives |
| S3/S5-dose1000, -dose2500 | T3-dose150, T3-dose300 | Smaller amounts of synthetic positives |

The balanced, dose and T6 scenarios were added after the first results and are exploratory.

### Classifiers and evaluation

- Main classifier: DenseNet-121 (TorchXRayVision, CheXpert-only weights to avoid leakage
  from NIH), class-balanced sampler, early stopping and Youden threshold on a real
  validation set, three seeds.
- Robustness check (tuberculosis, T1/T2/T3/T5): ResNet-50 and ConvNeXt-Tiny with ImageNet
  weights, one baseline per architecture.
- Metrics: AUROC (primary), AUPRC, recall, specificity, precision, F1, AP/PA breakdown.
- Confidence intervals: patient-level cluster bootstrap (pneumonia) or stratified image
  bootstrap (tuberculosis), 2,000 replicates, paired against the baseline.
- Shortcut diagnostic: mean predicted probability on held-out synthetic normal images
  compared with real normal images.
- Image quality: FID, KID (Inception and TorchXRayVision features), SSIM / MS-SSIM, IS,
  real-vs-synthetic detector, nearest-neighbour memorisation check.

## Main findings

- Pneumonia: no scenario with synthetic data outperforms the real-only baseline, and the
  cost grows with the amount of synthetic data. A classifier trained with RoentGen-v2
  positives labels almost every RoentGen-v2 image as pneumonia (a shortcut).
- Tuberculosis: RoentGen-v2 images lower external AUROC with all three classifiers.
  The gain from DC-GAN images depends on the classifier (positive with DenseNet-121 and
  ResNet-50, not with ConvNeXt-Tiny).
- Image-quality metrics did not predict usefulness for the classifier.

Results are indicative and exploratory, not clinical evidence.

## How to run

All notebooks are written for Kaggle Notebooks (GPU for training and generation; the
evaluation notebooks need no GPU and no internet).

1. Add the NIH ChestX-ray14 dataset as input and run `01` and `02` (the latter writes the
   split CSVs).
2. Add the output of `02` as input to `03a` and `03b1/03b2`, then run `03c` and `04a`.
3. Run the `04b` notebooks and `05`.
4. Tuberculosis: add Shenzhen and Montgomery as input, run `06a`, then `06b1/06b2`,
   `06c` (both sessions) and `06e`. Evaluate with `06d`: attach the outputs of `06a`,
   both `06c` sessions and the `06e` outputs, and set `BACKBONE` (`densenet`,
   `resnet50` or `convnext_tiny`) once per classifier.
5. `03b1/03b2` and `06b2` need access to `stanfordmimi/RoentGen-v2` on Hugging Face
   (gated; requires accepting the terms). Save your token as the Kaggle Secret `HF_TOKEN`.
   **Never write the token in a notebook or commit it.**

Approximate times on a Tesla T4: DC-GAN training 5-6 h, RoentGen-v2 generation about 10 h
for the pneumonia set (30-step DPM-Solver++), classifier scenarios minutes each.

## Limitations

Small number of positives (683 in the pneumonia training pool), single modality and two
applications, small tuberculosis test sets (166 internal, 138 external) with wide
intervals, no correction for multiple comparisons, RoentGen-v2 used zero-shot (it does not
depict tuberculosis recognisably) and trained on PA views only, differing native
resolutions (128 vs. 512 px), a detectable gap between real and synthetic images, and no
radiologist review of the synthetic images.
